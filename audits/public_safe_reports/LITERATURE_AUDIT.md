# Literature and priority assessment

Bhattacharya, Crane and Johnston prove transience above 1/2 under a more general growth assumption and give a dichotomy that leaves the exact threshold open. Volkov independently proves the super-square-root case with related one-time Fourier decay and a local limit argument. One-time summability alone does not give the transient range 1/4 < α ≤ 1/2. The distinction from integer-rounded steps is substantive. The targeted primary-source check through 2026-11-27 located no identical threshold or stronger theorem supplying it.

# Scope and prior-art audit, November 27, 2026

- Source checked: [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24568), v2 dated December 19, 2025, p. 6, immediately before Theorem 13, and the [author-hosted copy](https://tomjohnston.co.uk/papers/recurrence-transcience-and-anti-concentration-of-rademacher-random-walks.pdf). No later arXiv revision or journal version located by title, authors, or identifier. The quoted fragment appears in the latest arXiv version. The paper's weak recurrence permits a random bounded interval; topological recurrence means each nonempty open interval is visited infinitely often. No claim of an exact atom revisit.
- The same paper proves transience for steps a_n=n^(α+o(1)) with α>1/2, not on the addressed side of the exponent. Its example of weak recurrence at Θ(√n) does not have exact step sequence √n. Its Theorem 13 gives a dichotomy for exact powers but does not choose a side. The proof gives the side and separately supplies a direct upgrade.
- [Bhattacharya–Volkov, ALEA 20 (2023), 33–51](https://alea.impa.br/articles/v20/20-03.pdf) studies the same random-walk model and other examples; integer steps and integer-rounded powers do not transfer to this problem: balancing depends on exact smoothly varying weights and modulo effects disappear only with the real phase-spread estimate. Their logarithmic recurrence result does not order recurrence by step size. The [two-dimensional follow-up](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10959-026-01497-2) changes the step directions and target.
- The one-time bound is insufficient on the requested transitional range: the sum over n of n^(−α−1/2) diverges throughout α≤1/2. The second moment in the recurrent range requires uniform short-block conditioning, not just the marginals. Transience in the remaining range uses a first-hit stopping time and a conditional lower bound on a cluster, not a routine application of the usual first Borel–Cantelli argument to one-time events.
- Bounded searches for the exact excerpt, exact powers, weighted Rademacher returns, and exponent 1/4 located no full prior exact resolution. This is a bounded prior-art check, not peer review or a priority guarantee. No Lean proof is claimed.

- Closest newly posted follow-up: [Volkov, arXiv:2611.31508v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.31508v1#page=2), Theorem 1 and Lemmas 1–3, uses a related last-half phase-spread/local-limit calculation (compare our one-time estimate). Its transience uses one-time summability and assumes a logarithmic derivative strictly above 1/2. The one-time estimate and a local limit law still do not determine Q1600; the uniform short-block cell bound and stopping-time cluster mechanism address that gap. The sentence on p. 1 comparing α<1/2 and α≥1/2 applies to the rounded model just introduced, and the next paragraph explicitly distinguishes unrounded powers.

[Volkov, Theorem 1 and proof](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.31508v1), makes explicit the analogous one-time Fourier bound and local limit proof. Its stated uniform logarithmic derivative lower bound is strictly above 1/2. One-time bounds do not separate recurrence and transience in the source question.
