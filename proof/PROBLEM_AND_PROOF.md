# Q1600: exact recurrence threshold for real-power Rademacher walks

## Source and result

Satyaki Bhattacharya, Edward Crane, and Tom Johnston, *Recurrence, transience and anti-concentration of Rademacher random walks*, [arXiv v2, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.24568v2#page=6), paragraph before Theorem 13: “we have not determined the behaviour in the natural case where” the step size is the exact real power n^α, 0<α≤1/2. The [author's alternate copy](https://tomjohnston.co.uk/papers/recurrence-transcience-and-anti-concentration-of-rademacher-random-walks.pdf) retains this passage and Theorem 13. [Pinned catalog Q1600](https://github.com/10m-u/open-problems-catalog/blob/ca8d4d44ba3db527f19f28928c240168f545ecfe/problems/mathematics/probability-stochastic-processes-part-1.md#q1600).

Let ε_j be independent symmetric signs, and X_n=∑_{j=1}^n ε_j j^α, with no rounding. For 0<α≤1/4, almost surely every nonempty open interval is visited infinitely often. For 1/4<α≤1/2, almost surely |X_n|→∞. All constants below may depend on α and on the fixed interval radius; m and l are positive integers.

## Proof

**Block small-ball estimate.** Fix α∈(0,1/2] and an interval radius R. For m large and 1≤l≤m, let Y be the increment X_(m+l)−X_m. Uniformly in its interval center y,
P(|Y−y|≤R) ≤ C [m^−α l^−1/2 + min(l^−1/2,m^(1−α)l^−2)].
The l=1 case is immediate. For l≥2 use a rescaled squared sinc majorant of the interval, giving a constant times the integral over |t|≤T of |∏_{j=1}^l cos(t(m+j)^α)|, with T fixed. Explicitly, choose b>0 with bR≤1; a constant times (sin(bu)/(bu))² dominates the interval indicator and has nonnegative bounded transform supported on [−2b,2b]. Translate by y and apply Fourier inversion with the triangle inequality. Let p_j=|t|(m+j)^α, S=p_l−p_1 and A=((m+1)^α+(m+l)^α)/2. The gaps p_(j+1)−p_j are comparable to |t|m^(α−1) by the mean value theorem, and S ≍ |t|l m^(α−1).

Write d(u)=dist(u,πZ). For S≤1, ∑ d(p_j)^2 ≥ c l[d(|t|A)^2+S²]. If d(|t|A)≥S, the Lipschitz bound gives distance at least half the center distance throughout. Otherwise all p_j lie within 3S/2<π/2 of the same lattice point. The sum of squared distances is at least the sum of squared deviations from the mean. A positive fraction of pairs are separated by a constant times S because the gaps are comparable. The same bound holds for l=2 by directly using the endpoints. If S>1, then ∫_(p₁)^(p_l)d(u)^2du≥cS: the neighborhoods of radius 1/16 around πZ occupy a uniformly bounded fraction of such an interval. Put h=max gap. Since d² is Lipschitz, the integral is at most h∑d(p_j)^2+O(hS). On |t|≤T, h tends uniformly to zero as m→∞. Therefore ∑d(p_j)^2≥c S/h≥c'l.

Use |cos u|≤exp(−c d(u)^2). On S>1 the contribution to the integral is O(e^−cl), absorbable into the displayed block bound because m≥l and min(l^−1/2,m^(1−α)l^−2)≥l^(−1−α). On S≤1 integrate exp[−cl d(tA)^2−c t²l³m^(2α−2)]. Partition into the cells |tA−jπ|≤π/2. Each d-term integral is O(m^−α l^−1/2). In cell j≠0 the second term contributes at most exp(−c j²l³/m²). There are O(m^α) nonzero cells on |t|≤T and the Gaussian sum over j≥1 is O(m/l^(3/2)). Thus the integral is bounded by Cm^−α l^−1/2[1+min(m^α,m/l^(3/2))], as needed.

**One-time estimate.** On using only the last floor(n/2) factors, the phase estimates also give |E exp(itX_n)|≤exp[−cn min(1,t²n^(2α))] for |t|≤T. Thus for any fixed R the one-time small-ball probability is O(n^−α−1/2), uniformly in center. For a lower bound at center 0, take F(x)=(1−x²/R²)(sin x/x)^4, with R large enough that ∫F>0. This integrable minorant is ≤1_[−R,R] and its bounded continuous Fourier transform has support in [−4,4] by the sinc convolution and the differentiation identity. Fourier inversion, t=z n^−α−1/2, and the last-half bound show that n^(α+1/2) E F(X_n) tends to a positive constant. Indeed pointwise the scaled characteristic function tends to exp(−z²/[2(2α+1)]) by Taylor expansion of log cos and ∑j^(2α)∼n^(2α+1)/(2α+1). The rescaled majorant is Gaussian until |z| reaches √n up to fixed factors and exponentially small across the rest of the frequency interval. Hence P(|X_n|≤R)≍n^−α−1/2 for this R.

**Recurrence.** Put Z_N=∑_{n≤N}1_{|X_n|≤R}. Then E Z_N ≍ N^(1/2−α) for α≤1/4. For joint hits at m<n with n≥2m, the conditional probability of the later hit is O(n^−α−1/2) by using the final half of the increments and conditioning on those before. This part of E Z_N² is O((E Z_N)²). For m<n<2m the block estimate applies with l=n−m. Summing its right side over l≤m gives O(m^(1/2−α)+m^((1−α)/3)). For the second term split at m^(2(1−α)/3). When α≤1/4 the first power dominates, including equality at the endpoint. Multiply by P(|X_m|≤R) and sum over m≤N to obtain E Z_N²=O(N^(1−2α)). Paley–Zygmund gives a positive constant lower bound for P(Z_N≥E Z_N/2) for all large N. The deterministic threshold tends to infinity, so by monotonicity of Z_N this proves positive probability of infinitely many visits. The existence of some bounded interval visited infinitely often is a tail event: for each M it is equivalent to bounded infinitely often for the sums starting after M, since X_M is a finite shift. Kolmogorov's zero-one law gives weak recurrence. The source's Theorem 13 then gives topological recurrence, since j^α is unbounded and j^α−(j−1)^α→0.

**Transience.** Fix R and stop at the first hit of [−R,R] in [N,2N]. Conditional on a hit at m, use the next even l≤cN^(2(1−α)/3) steps and condition their signs to have sum zero, an event of probability ≳l^−1/2. On that event the increment has mean zero and variance l/(l−1) times the sum of squared deviations of the step weights from their mean. The weights have range O(N^(α−1)l), so the variance is O(N^(2α−2)l³). Choose c small enough for conditional probability at least 1/2 of an increment in [−1,1]. These times all lie in [N,3N] for large N. Summing over these even l gives expected enlarged-interval visits ≳N^((1−α)/3) given a hit. Unconditionally the one-time upper estimate bounds the enlarged-interval count in [N,3N] by O(N^(1/2−α)) in expectation, including at α=1/2. The probability of a hit in [N,2N] is therefore O(N^((1−4α)/6)). For α>1/4 this is dyadically summable, so first Borel–Cantelli and a countable union over integral R prove transience.

## Independent upgrade to topological recurrence

Here is a version of the lower-bound argument that does not require Theorem 13. For any fixed interval I=[y−r,y+r] with r>0, choose B large so F_I(x)=(1−(x−y)²/r²)(sin(B(x−y))/(B(x−y)))⁴ has positive integral. This follows by changing variables z=B(x−y), since both the zeroth and second moments of sinc(z)^4 are finite. This is an integrable minorant for the interval indicator with bounded, compactly supported Fourier transform; multiplying by the quadratic does not enlarge the Fourier support.

Condition on any first M signs, M fixed. The same rescaled Fourier inversion argument applies to the remaining independent signs, using the same last-half decay once n>2M. The omitted first M weights and the deterministic shift X_M vanish in the characteristic-function limit on the scaled t=z n^(−α−1/2) axis. The limiting scaled expectation of F_I has the same strictly positive leading constant for every fixed prefix. The one-time upper and the joint second-moment bounds are uniform in that prefix, using only observations after 2M. Thus for the count of visits to I after 2M and up to N, its first moment for all sufficiently large N is bounded below by a fixed positive constant times N^(1/2−α), and its second moment is bounded above by a fixed multiple of N^(1−2α), with constants independent of M and the particular prefix. The threshold on N is allowed to depend on the prefix. For the joint estimate with n≥2m use the last floor(n/2) factors; the starting index differs from half by at most one, so the same phase calculation applies. Paley–Zygmund as above gives P(I visited infinitely often | first M signs)≥c_I>0 for every fixed M. These conditional probabilities converge almost surely to the indicator of infinitely many visits by bounded martingale convergence, so that indicator is 1 almost surely. Intersect over closed rational subintervals, each with positive length.

## Why the threshold differs from one-time summability

Across a length-l block starting near m, almost constant weights make balanced signs return on scale 1 when l is of order m^(2(1−α)/3). The first moment in a dyadic epoch grows at scale N^(1/2−α), but a single hit initiates on average order N^((1−α)/3) enlarged-interval returns. The exponents cross at α=1/4. In the recurrence half, the frequency-cell estimate makes this same short-time scale enter the joint-hit upper bound, with no logarithmic loss at the endpoint.
